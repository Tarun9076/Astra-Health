## What happens
On the way to Mars crews are weightless for months, then live and work at about three-eighths of Earth's gravity on the surface. Muscles, bones, the inner ear (balance) and the heart and blood vessels all adjust to each new gravity level, and each transition can bring dizziness, clumsiness or motion sickness for a while.

## What astronauts can do
Do the prescribed exercise sessions every week, move carefully for the first days after a gravity change, and stand up slowly. Report balance problems or falls.

## When to contact the flight surgeon
If dizziness, poor coordination or nausea lasts beyond the first days after a transition, or after any fall or fainting. Use the Report button or tell the crew medical officer (CMO) if the problem is sudden, severe or getting worse. The app also flags it for the flight surgeon on the next link window.
