## What happens
Beyond Earth's protective magnetic field and atmosphere, crews are exposed to galactic cosmic rays and, during solar storms, bursts of solar particles. NASA counts space radiation among the most hazardous parts of spaceflight. It cannot be seen or felt. The main long-term concern NASA lists is a higher lifetime cancer risk; very large doses in a short time can also cause acute illness.

## What astronauts can do
Wear your personal dosimeter at all times so your dose is recorded. During a solar particle event, go to the shielded shelter as soon as the alert appears and stay until mission control gives the all-clear. Follow the planned limits for time outside the habitat.

## How ASTRA HEALTH tracks it
The app records your daily dose (total and neutron channels), compares it with the habitat's normal, raises an Urgent alert on a sudden spike, and tracks the share of the mission dose limit used so far.

## When to contact the flight surgeon
Always after a solar particle event, or if you feel nauseous, unusually tired or unwell after a high-dose day. Use the Report button or tell the crew medical officer (CMO) if the problem is sudden, severe or getting worse. The app also flags it for the flight surgeon on the next link window.
