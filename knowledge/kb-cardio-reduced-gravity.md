## What happens
Without normal gravity, body fluids shift toward the head and the heart and blood vessels adapt to a lighter workload. Over time this reduces cardiovascular fitness, and NASA lists cardiovascular adaptations as a spaceflight risk. A resting heart rate that rises above your own normal for several days usually reflects stress on the body, such as poor sleep, dehydration, illness, fatigue or hard training without recovery.

## What astronauts can do
Recheck resting heart rate on waking, before coffee or exercise. Drink enough, protect your sleep, and keep up the planned aerobic and resistance exercise.

## When to contact the flight surgeon
Immediately for chest pain or pressure, fainting, a racing or irregular heartbeat at rest, or severe shortness of breath. Otherwise, if resting heart rate stays well above your normal for more than a few days.
