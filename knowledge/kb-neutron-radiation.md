## What happens
When space radiation hits spacecraft walls and other material, it can produce neutrons. The Canadian Space Agency reports that neutrons make up about 30 % of the radiation on the International Space Station and are especially harmful to living tissue, so they need specialised protection and measurement.

## How it is measured
Canada flew earlier radiation experiments on the ISS and used small bubble detectors. It is now developing the Canadian Active Neutron Spectrometer (CANS), a compact instrument that measures neutron exposure continuously and on its own. The same technology helps on Earth, for example in cancer therapy and protecting aircrews.

## How ASTRA HEALTH tracks it
Each dosimeter reading is stored with separate total and neutron channels, so the share of neutrons can be followed over the mission.
